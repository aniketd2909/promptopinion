1. Sore Throat / Possible Strep

Doctor: Hey, good morning. What's been going on?
Patient: Uh yeah so my throat's been killing me for like, I guess, three days now.
Doctor: Three days, okay.
Patient: Yeah and then last night I started feeling, like, really hot. I checked and it was 101 point 8.
Doctor: Got it. Any cough? Runny nose?
Patient: Not really a cough. Maybe a little stuffed up, but not like a full cold or anything.
Doctor: Any trouble swallowing?
Patient: Yeah, it hurts, mostly on this side, the left side. Even water kinda hurts.
Doctor: Any nausea, vomiting, rash?
Patient: No, none of that.
Doctor: Anybody around you sick?
Patient: My kid had strep last week, so that's why my wife was like you probably need to get checked.
Doctor: Makes sense. Any medication allergies?
Patient: Nope.
Doctor: What have you taken so far?
Patient: Tylenol, cough drops, warm tea, the usual. Helps for like an hour and then it's back.
Doctor: Okay. Let me take a look at your throat and we'll probably do a rapid strep test.
Patient: Okay.

2. Diabetes Follow-Up With Poor Control

Doctor: Hi, how've you been since the last visit?
Patient: Honestly, not great with the sugars.
Doctor: Tell me more.
Patient: In the mornings they're still high. Like 180, 190, sometimes over 200 if I eat bad the night before.
Doctor: Are you checking every morning?
Patient: Most mornings. Not every single day, but yeah, pretty often.
Doctor: And you're still on metformin and glipizide?
Patient: Yeah, metformin a thousand twice a day, and then the glipizide in the morning.
Doctor: Taking them consistently?
Patient: Mostly. I forget the nighttime metformin sometimes. Maybe once, twice a week.
Doctor: Any low blood sugars? Shaky, sweaty, lightheaded?
Patient: No, not really. If anything I feel the opposite, like thirsty all the time.
Doctor: More urination too?
Patient: Yeah, especially overnight, which is annoying.
Doctor: How's your diet been?
Patient: Bad. I'm on the road a lot, fast food, gas station stuff, you know.
Doctor: Any numbness or tingling in the feet?
Patient: A little tingling in my toes, yeah.
Doctor: Vision changes?
Patient: No, vision seems okay.
Doctor: Do you remember your last A1c?
Patient: I think it was 8 point something. 8.9 maybe?
Doctor: Okay. We'll recheck labs and talk about adjusting the meds.
Patient: Yeah, I figured.

3. Asthma Flare During Allergy Season

Doctor: So what's going on with your breathing?
Patient: I've been wheezing a lot this week. Like way more than normal.
Doctor: When did it start?
Patient: Maybe five, six days ago? Around then.
Doctor: Any shortness of breath?
Patient: Yeah, especially stairs. I walked up to my apartment and had to stop for a sec.
Doctor: Using your rescue inhaler?
Patient: A ton. More than I should, probably. Four or five times a day.
Doctor: What about your daily inhaler?
Patient: Uh, I ran out. Like two weeks ago maybe.
Doctor: Which one was that?
Patient: Flovent.
Doctor: Any nighttime symptoms?
Patient: Yeah, I woke up coughing a couple times this week.
Doctor: Fever? Chills?
Patient: No fever. Little bit of runny nose though.
Doctor: Any smoking or vaping?
Patient: No.
Doctor: What usually triggers your asthma?
Patient: Pollen for sure. And whatever's in the air right now is awful.
Doctor: Any hospitalizations recently for asthma?
Patient: No, not since I was a kid.
Doctor: Have you needed steroids in the last year?
Patient: Last spring they gave me prednisone when I had a flare.
Doctor: Okay, let's listen to your lungs and check your oxygen.
Patient: Sounds good.

5. Knee Injury

Doctor: What happened to the knee?
Patient: I twisted it getting out of my truck. It sounds dumb, but I stepped weird and it just, like, shot pain right through it.
Doctor: Which knee?
Patient: The right one.
Doctor: When was this?
Patient: Three days ago.
Doctor: Did you fall down?
Patient: No, I caught myself.
Doctor: Did you hear or feel a pop?
Patient: I don't think I heard anything, maybe felt something weird, I don't know. It happened fast.
Doctor: Has it been swollen?
Patient: Yeah, that night it puffed up pretty good. It's a little better now but still swollen.
Doctor: Are you able to walk?
Patient: Yeah but I'm limping. If I turn wrong it kind of grabs.
Doctor: Where's the pain exactly?
Patient: More on the inside part.
Doctor: Any locking, catching, or giving way?
Patient: It feels kind of wobbly, like it might buckle.
Doctor: What have you done for it?
Patient: Ice, ibuprofen, and one of those cheap braces from CVS.
Doctor: Any prior knee injuries?
Patient: Not serious. Sprained it in high school playing football, but no surgery or anything.
Doctor: Pain scale right now?
Patient: Sitting here maybe a three. Walking, like a six.
Doctor: Okay, let me examine it and we'll see if you need an X-ray or MRI.
Patient: Alright.
